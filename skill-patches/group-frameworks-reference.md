# Group Curriculum Frameworks - Skill Reference

## When Planning a Day

1. **Start with the 600-Second Commitment**: 10-minute daily self-meeting. Use Eisenhower Matrix to sort tasks, SMART Goals to make them actionable.

2. **Check the Dopamenu**: Before planning, identify what Starters or Sides can be woven into the day to sustain dopamine. Don't rely on willpower alone.

3. **Build in transition buffers**: ADHD brains struggle with task-switching. Allow 10-15 minutes between blocks.

4. **Pre-plan for Stage 3 of the Shame Cycle**: When focus drifts (it will), have a list of smaller backup tasks ready to switch to. This isn't failure - it's the plan.

## When Overwhelmed or Stuck

1. **Run HALT**: Hungry? Angry? Lonely? Tired? Address the need before pushing through.

2. **Recognize the Shame Cycle stage**: Name which stage you're in. Naming it externalizes it.
   - Stage 4 (Feeling like a failure): Ask "Who told me that?" - trace the negative message to its source.
   - Stage 5 (Seeking instant gratification): Check if the comfort behavior is time-sucking or recoverable.
   - Stage 6 (End-of-day regret): Distinguish shame from guilt. Guilt is workable; shame needs compassion.

3. **Use Goblin.tools** to break the stuck task into subtasks. Often the task isn't too hard - it's too vague.

4. **Body-double**: Ask someone to work alongside you, go to a public space, or put on a YouTube video of someone doing a similar task.

## When Emotions Are Running Hot

1. **Emotions are data, not commands.** Get curious: what is this emotion trying to show you?

2. **Ride the wave**: Remind yourself that feelings are temporary. You will not always feel this way.

3. **Externalize**: Journal it, text a friend, use the feelings wheel to name it precisely.

4. **Avoid making decisions or sending messages** when flooded. The emotional hyperarousal associated with ADHD means the intensity you feel may not match the situation's actual severity.

## When Planning for Sleep

1. **Daytime = productive time. Nighttime is not.** If you're stealing sleep time to do things you procrastinated, that's a planning problem, not a sleep problem.

2. **Wind-down before bed** - don't use bedtime as wind-down time. Give yourself leisure before bed so you're not tempted to use sleep time for it.

3. **Phone out of arm's reach.** Prevents revenge bedtime procrastination and forces you to physically get up for the alarm.

## Key Vocabulary

- **Procrastivity**: Choosing a less important task to avoid a harder one (Ramsay, 2023)
- **Messytasking**: Attempting two things at once and making a mess of both (Crowell, 2022)
- **Emotional hyperarousal**: Especially passionate internal reactions associated with ADHD
- **Revenge bedtime procrastination**: Staying up late for leisure because the day didn't feel like "yours"
- **Body-doubling**: Working alongside another person for motivational co-presence (Barkley, 2023)
