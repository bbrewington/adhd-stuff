# Tools & Apps Index

Recommended tools and apps referenced in ADHD group curriculum and discussion. Organized by what they help with.

---

## Task Management & Planning

### Goblin.tools
- **What:** AI-based tool suite for neurodivergent productivity
- **Key feature:** Magic To-Do - breaks tasks into smaller, manageable subtasks automatically
- **Use when:** A task feels too big or vague to start (Shame Cycle Stage 2-3)
- **URL:** https://goblin.tools

### Eisenhower Matrix (framework, not an app)
- **What:** 2x2 grid for prioritizing tasks by urgency and importance
- **Use when:** Daily planning, the 600-second commitment
- **See:** frameworks.md > Eisenhower Matrix

## Focus & Distraction Management

### Brick
- **What:** Phone-blocking app that physically prevents access to selected apps
- **Use when:** Need to create physical barriers to distraction (Focus skills)
- **Platform:** iOS, Android
- **URL:** https://www.getbrick.app

### Pomodoro Timer
- **What:** Time-boxing technique - 25 minutes of focused work, 5-minute break
- **Use when:** Sustaining effort on a single task, mono-tasking
- **Variations:** Many apps available; any timer works. Adjust intervals to fit (some ADHD folks prefer 15/5 or 45/15)
- **Note:** Pairs well with body-doubling

### Screen Time / App Limits (built-in)
- **What:** iOS Screen Time / Android Digital Wellbeing
- **Use when:** Creating time barriers on social media and other time-sucking apps
- **Tip:** Set daily limits; the friction of the "time's up" prompt is often enough

## Sleep & Alertness

### Hatch Alarm Clock
- **What:** Sunrise alarm clock with customizable light and sound routines
- **Use when:** Building a nighttime routine, waking up more gently
- **Why it helps:** Light-based wake-up supports circadian rhythm; programmable wind-down routine creates structure
- **URL:** https://www.hatch.co

### Headspace - Sleep Casts
- **What:** Guided sleep stories within the Headspace meditation app
- **Use when:** Difficulty shutting the mind off at night, late-night rumination
- **Alternative:** Podcast "Nothing Much Happens" (free, similar concept)
- **URL:** https://www.headspace.com

### Sunlight Exposure (no app needed)
- **What:** 10-15 minutes of bright light (ideally sunlight) early in the day
- **Use when:** Daily, as early as possible after waking
- **Why it helps:** Increases alertness, helps balance circadian rhythm

## Emotional Regulation

### Feelings Wheel
- **What:** Visual tool mapping emotions from core (center) to specific (outer ring)
- **Use when:** Daily emotional check-ins, identifying what you're feeling beyond "good" or "bad"
- **Source:** Trigg (2019) - www.feelingswheel.com
- **Core emotions:** Anger, Disgust, Sad, Happy, Surprise, Fear

### Emotion Tracker (spreadsheet template)
- **What:** Simple log tracking: when / trigger / feeling / reaction / consequences
- **Use when:** Building awareness of emotional patterns over time
- **Source:** How to ADHD video on emotional dysregulation
- **Template:** See `templates/emotion-tracker.md`

### Coping Skills Log (spreadsheet template)
- **What:** Before/after tracker for practicing coping skills
- **Columns:** Situation, Thoughts, Emotions (intensity 1-10), Results, Emotions after (intensity 1-10)
- **Use when:** Practicing a new coping technique and want to measure its impact
- **Source:** TherapistAid.com (2023)
- **Template:** See `templates/coping-skills-log.md`

## Body-Doubling

### What It Is
Sustaining motivation by working alongside another person who is engaged in their own activity (Barkley, 2023). Not collaboration - just co-presence.

### How to Do It
- Ask someone: "Will you body double for/with me?"
- Go to a public space (coffee shop, library)
- Play a podcast or YouTube video of someone doing the same activity
- Virtual body-doubling services exist (Focusmate, Flow Club)

### When to Use
Best for tasks that are lengthy, repetitive, or boring.

## Accessibility & Processing

### Voice Typing
- **What:** Dictation built into Apple and Google devices
- **Use when:** Writing feels like a barrier; externalizing ideas verbally is easier
- **Where:** iOS/macOS dictation, Google Docs voice typing, most mobile keyboards

### Alternate Information Formats
- Request recordings of meetings or presentations
- Ask for visuals or handouts alongside verbal information
- Use text-to-speech for reading (most e-readers and browsers support this)
